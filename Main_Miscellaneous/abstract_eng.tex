\begin{englishabstract}

With the rapid development of smart wearable devices and artificial intelligence technologies, modeling human motion states using non-visual sensors has gradually become a research hotspot. Among them, Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) can capture high-frequency kinematic information of human motion, while Channel State Information (CSI) in WiFi signals can reflect the perturbations caused by human movement in the wireless propagation environment. Compared with vision-based sensors, such sensing approaches are more privacy-friendly, flexible to deploy, and robust to environmental variations. However, several challenges remain in practical applications. First, heterogeneous sensor modalities exhibit significant differences in signal representation and feature distribution, making effective multimodal alignment and fusion difficult. Second, variations in motion habits and physical characteristics among different subjects lead to performance degradation in cross-subject scenarios. Third, existing human pose reconstruction methods mainly focus on reconstructing poses at the current time step, lacking the capability to model future poses.

To address these challenges, this paper focuses on two tasks: human activity recognition and human pose reconstruction. For the human activity recognition task, we propose a multimodal alignment and fusion-based method, termed WIHAR, to tackle the difficulty of fusing heterogeneous features from IMU and WiFi CSI data, as well as the limited cross-subject generalization ability. Specifically, a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy \cite{radford2021learning} is employed to map IMU kinematic features and WiFi CSI radio-frequency sensing features into a shared semantic space, and a cross-attention mechanism is introduced to enable feature interaction between the two modalities. Furthermore, a prototype-based feature alignment strategy is adopted to map target-domain features into the semantic structure of the source domain, thereby effectively mitigating cross-subject distribution discrepancies. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset \cite{lan2025xrf} demonstrate that WIHAR achieves an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06\%. In cross-subject settings, the model attains an initial average accuracy of 72.57\%, which can be further improved to 90.99\% with a small amount of target-subject data for fine-tuning.

For the human pose reconstruction task, we propose AIPose, an autoregressive modeling-based method for both pose reconstruction and prediction, to address the lack of temporal prediction capability in existing approaches. Using IMU data as input, AIPose constructs a closed-loop framework consisting of an IMU feature extraction module, a pose reconstruction module, and an IMU prediction module. The model predicts future IMU sequences based on current IMU inputs and further utilizes them for future pose prediction, thereby achieving both current pose reconstruction and future pose forecasting. Experimental results show that on the XRFV2 dataset, AIPose achieves a mean per-joint position error (MPJPE) of 50.56 for current pose reconstruction and 56.29 for future pose prediction.
