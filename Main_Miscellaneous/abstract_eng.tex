    With the proliferation of smart wearable devices and wireless sensing technologies, collaborative sensing based on Inertial Measurement Units (IMU) and WiFi has emerged as a focal point in human health monitoring. IMU and WiFi offer inherent advantages such as low cost, ease of deployment, and robust privacy protection. However, in practical applications, the representation capability of a single sensing modality remains limited under complex environments. Meanwhile, significant variations in physiological characteristics and movement habits among individuals lead to domain shifts, causing severe performance degradation of pre-trained models on unseen subjects. Furthermore, existing research on human pose reconstruction primarily focuses on static restoration at the current moment, lacking the capability for dynamic prediction of future trends. To address these core issues—difficulty in heterogeneous data fusion, generalization bottlenecks due to individual differences, and the lack of predictability in pose reconstruction—this thesis investigates a multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition method and an autoregressive-based human pose reconstruction and prediction method.

    In the study of multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition (WICTIVITY), to address the challenges of substantial differences in physical properties, feature alignment difficulties, and deep fusion between IMU and WiFi data, this thesis designs a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy. This strategy explicitly reduces the distance between IMU dynamic features and WiFi spatial field features within a latent semantic space, thereby overcoming the semantic gap between heterogeneous data and achieving deep complementarity through a cross-attention mechanism.  On this basis, a multi-task classification network is constructed to achieve collaborative perception of human activities, user identities, and environments within a unified framework. Experimental results demonstrate that WICTIVITY effectively mines complementary multimodal information on the XRFV2 dataset, achieving an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06\%, while user identity and scene recognition accuracies reach 99.93\% and 99.99\%, respectively. Regarding the performance degradation in cross-subject recognition, this thesis further proposes a few-shot adaptation strategy based on global semantic anchor alignment. This strategy introduces a lightweight bottleneck adapter and utilizes a prototypical alignment loss function to guide the feature distribution of target subjects toward various activity feature centers learned from the source domain, effectively calibrating the feature offset caused by individual differences.  Results indicate that the model's initial average recognition rate on unseen subjects is 72.57\%. Through fine-tuning with only 5 to 10 target domain samples, the average accuracy increases to 78.82\% and eventually stabilizes at 90.99\% as the sample size increases.

    In the study of autoregressive-based human pose reconstruction and prediction (AIPose), to address the lack of dynamic predictive capability in existing methods, this thesis proposes a method that extends pose reconstruction from static mapping to dynamic temporal generation. This method constructs a closed-loop generative architecture comprising IMU feature extraction, pose reconstruction, and IMU signal prediction.  By feeding the predicted future IMU data back into the feature extraction module, continuous reconstruction and look-ahead prediction of human poses are achieved. Experimental results show that AIPose achieves centimeter-level pose reconstruction on the XRFV2 dataset and possesses stable long-term pose prediction capabilities. The mean per-joint position error for current pose reconstruction is 49.35 mm, and the prediction error for future poses is 55.19 mm.
