    With the rapid development of wearable devices and wireless sensing technologies, human behavior perception based on non-visual sensors has attracted increasing attention. Among them, inertial measurement units (IMUs) can capture high-frequency kinematic information of human motion, while the channel state information (CSI) of WiFi signals can reflect the disturbances of wireless signal propagation caused by human activities. Compared with vision-based approaches, these sensing modalities offer advantages such as privacy preservation, flexible deployment, and strong environmental adaptability. However, several challenges remain in practical applications. On the one hand, multimodal sensor data exhibit significant differences in signal forms and feature distributions, making effective multimodal fusion difficult. On the other hand, variations in motion patterns among different subjects often lead to performance degradation in cross-subject scenarios. In addition, most existing IMU-based human pose reconstruction methods focus on estimating the pose at the current time step, while lacking the ability to model future motion trends.

    To address these challenges, this thesis investigates two tasks: human activity recognition and human pose reconstruction. For multimodal human activity recognition, a multimodal fusion framework named WICTIVITY is proposed to address the difficulties in heterogeneous feature alignment between IMU and WiFi data as well as the limited cross-subject generalization ability. Specifically, a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy is employed to map IMU kinematic features and WiFi radio-frequency sensing features into a shared semantic space, and a cross-attention mechanism is introduced to enable deep interaction between the two modalities. Furthermore, a prototype-based few-shot adaptation strategy is adopted to mitigate cross-subject distribution shifts by aligning the features of the target user with class prototypes learned from the source domain. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset demonstrate that WICTIVITY achieves an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06% in the multimodal setting. In cross-subject experiments, the model achieves an average accuracy of 72.08% on unseen subjects, which improves to 87.29% after fine-tuning with only about 60 seconds of data from the target user.

    For the human pose reconstruction task, an autoregressive pose reconstruction and prediction framework named AIPose is proposed to address the lack of temporal prediction capability in existing approaches. The proposed method uses only IMU signals as input and constructs a closed-loop architecture consisting of an IMU feature extraction module, a pose reconstruction module, and an IMU prediction module. By predicting future IMU signals from historical IMU sequences through an autoregressive mechanism and feeding the predicted signals back to the reconstruction module, the framework enables both current pose reconstruction and future pose prediction. Experimental results show that AIPose achieves centimeter-level pose reconstruction accuracy on the XRFV2 dataset, with a mean per-joint position error of 49.35 mm for current pose reconstruction and 55.19 mm for future pose prediction.
