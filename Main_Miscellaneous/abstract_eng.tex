\begin{englishabstract}

With the development of intelligent wearable devices and artificial intelligence technologies, human motion state modeling based on non-visual sensors has gradually become an important research direction in intelligent sensing. Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) can capture acceleration and angular velocity during human motion, while Channel State Information (CSI) in WiFi signals can reflect disturbances caused by human motion in the wireless propagation environment. These modalities have advantages in privacy protection, flexible deployment, and environmental adaptability. However, existing methods still suffer from limitations in multimodal fusion, cross-subject generalization, and future state prediction. To address these issues, this thesis investigates two tasks, namely human activity recognition and human pose reconstruction, and further extends activity semantic recognition to human motion state modeling and prediction.

For the human activity recognition task, to address the significant differences in signal forms and feature distributions between IMU and WiFi CSI, which make effective multimodal fusion difficult and limit cross-subject generalization ability, a multimodal alignment and fusion-based human activity recognition method, termed WIHAR (WiFi-IMU Human Activity Recognition), is proposed. Specifically, a dual-branch temporal encoding network is first employed to extract features from the two modalities, and bidirectional cross-modal contrastive learning is introduced to map IMU and WiFi CSI features into a shared semantic space, thereby reducing the representation gap between heterogeneous modalities. Subsequently, a cross-attention mechanism is applied to enable fine-grained feature interaction across modalities and enhance the discriminative capability of the fused representation. To alleviate performance degradation in cross-subject scenarios, a class-prototype-based cross-subject adaptation strategy is further designed to guide target-domain features toward the shared semantic space. Experimental results show that WIHAR achieves an accuracy of 95.81\% on the XRFV2 dataset. Under cross-subject settings, the average accuracy is improved from 72.57\% to 90.99\% with a small amount of target-subject data for fine-tuning.

For the human pose reconstruction task, to overcome the limitation that existing methods mainly focus on current pose reconstruction while lacking the ability to predict future poses, an autoregressive modeling-based human pose reconstruction and prediction method, named AIPose (Autoregressive IMU Pose Reconstruction), is proposed. Taking IMU sequences as input, AIPose establishes a unified modeling framework composed of an IMU feature extraction module, a pose reconstruction module, an IMU prediction module, and an autoregressive modeling mechanism. Specifically, the current IMU sequence is used to reconstruct the current human pose while simultaneously predicting the future IMU sequence. The predicted IMU sequence is then utilized in an autoregressive manner for future pose reconstruction, thereby enabling future pose prediction. Experimental results show that, on the XRFV2 dataset, AIPose achieves a Mean Per Joint Position Error of 52.20\,px for current pose reconstruction and 57.36\,px for future pose prediction.

Overall, this thesis proposes WIHAR for human activity recognition and AIPose for pose reconstruction and prediction, providing a feasible technical path toward privacy-preserving and deployable non-visual human sensing in complex indoor environments.
