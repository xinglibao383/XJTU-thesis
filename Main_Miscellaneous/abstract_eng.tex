\begin{englishabstract}

    With the rapid development of wearable devices and artificial intelligence technologies, modeling human motion states based on non-visual sensors has gradually become a research hotspot. Compared with traditional visual sensing, non-visual sensing offers advantages such as privacy preservation, flexible deployment, and strong environmental adaptability. In particular, inertial measurement units (IMUs) can capture high-frequency kinematic information of human motion, while channel state information (CSI) from WiFi signals reflects the perturbations caused by human movement in wireless propagation environments. However, these methods still face several challenges in practical applications: significant differences in signal formats and feature distributions across sensor modalities make multimodal feature alignment and fusion difficult; variations in motion habits and body characteristics across subjects limit model generalization in cross-subject scenarios; moreover, existing human pose reconstruction methods mainly focus on current pose estimation and lack the ability to predict future poses. To address these issues, this work investigates two key tasks: human activity recognition and human pose reconstruction.

    For the human activity recognition task, to tackle the strong heterogeneity of IMU and WiFi CSI data and the limited cross-subject generalization, we propose WIHAR, a multimodal alignment and fusion-based recognition method. By incorporating a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy~\cite{radford2021learning}, WIHAR maps kinematic features from IMUs and radio-frequency perception features from WiFi CSI into a unified semantic space, and employs a cross-attention mechanism to enable deep feature interactions across modalities, thereby enhancing feature representation. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset show that WIHAR achieves an accuracy of 96.06\% in human activity recognition. Furthermore, we design a prototype-based feature alignment strategy to reduce the gap between target-domain features and source-domain class prototypes, mitigating the generalization limitation across subjects. With a small amount of target-subject data for fine-tuning, the model’s average accuracy improves from 72.57\% to 90.99\%.
    
    For the human pose reconstruction task, to address the lack of future pose prediction in existing methods, we propose AIPose, a human pose reconstruction and prediction approach based on autoregressive modeling. The method takes IMU sequences as input and constructs a closed-loop structure comprising an IMU feature extraction module, a pose reconstruction module, and an IMU prediction module. Future IMU sequences are predicted in an autoregressive manner and used as intermediate representations for future pose estimation. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset indicate that AIPose achieves a Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE) of 47.82 for current pose reconstruction and 54.11 for future pose prediction.

\end{englishabstract}
