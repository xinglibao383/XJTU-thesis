    With the rapid development of artificial intelligence and Internet of Things (IoT) technologies, coupled with the growing demand for daily health monitoring, smart wearable devices equipped with multiple sensors have proliferated. As key sensing components, the Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) and WiFi have demonstrated great potential in the fields of human activity recognition and pose reconstruction due to their low hardware cost, easy accessibility, and privacy-friendly characteristics. However, in practical applications, single-modal sensing faces limitations: IMUs lack environmental awareness, while WiFi signals are susceptible to environmental noise interference. Furthermore, significant differences in physical characteristics and data distribution between the two modalities pose challenges for deep multimodal fusion. Additionally, existing research on human pose estimation is mostly limited to the static reconstruction of the current moment, ignoring the temporal continuity and intrinsic laws of human motion, which results in a lack of predictive capability for future poses. To address these issues, this thesis conducts research on multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition and autoregressive-based human pose reconstruction and prediction, proposing innovative algorithmic models suitable for a wide range of daily scenarios.

    For the human activity recognition task, addressing the difficulties in aligning heterogeneous sensor data features and the limited sensing dimensions of single-task models, this thesis proposes a multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition method (WICTIVITY). In terms of architecture, WICTIVITY adopts the Mamba model as the core feature extractor to enhance the modeling efficiency and capability for long-sequence data. Simultaneously, a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy is introduced to explicitly align feature representations of different modalities in the semantic space, combined with a cross-attention mechanism to achieve deep feature fusion. Furthermore, a multi-task classification network is constructed to achieve simultaneous recognition of activity, identity, and scene. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset demonstrate that WICTIVITY effectively mines complementary multimodal information, achieving an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06\%, an identity recognition accuracy of 99.93\%, and a scene recognition accuracy of 99.99\%, fully validating the effectiveness of the proposed method.

    For the human pose reconstruction task, addressing the lack of prediction capability in existing methods, this thesis proposes an autoregressive mechanism-based human pose reconstruction and prediction method (AIPose), aiming to extend pose reconstruction from a static mapping to a dynamic temporal generation problem. AIPose integrates an IMU feature extractor, a pose reconstructor, and an IMU predictor to construct a closed-loop autoregressive architecture. By feeding the generated future IMU data back into the IMU feature extractor, the model achieves continuous reconstruction and anticipatory prediction of human poses. Experimental results indicate that AIPose achieves centimeter-level pose reconstruction on the XRFV2 dataset and possesses excellent pose prediction capabilities. The mean position error for the reconstructed pose at the current moment is 49.35 mm, and the error for the predicted future pose is 55.19 mm, fully validating the effectiveness of the method.
