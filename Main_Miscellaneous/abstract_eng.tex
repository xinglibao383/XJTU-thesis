\begin{englishabstract}

With the development of wearable devices and wireless sensing technologies, human behavior sensing based on non-visual sensors has become an active research topic. Inertial measurement units (IMUs) can capture high-frequency kinematic information of human motion, while the channel state information (CSI) of WiFi signals can reflect disturbances in wireless propagation caused by human activities. Compared with vision-based sensors, these sensing modalities are more privacy-preserving, flexible in deployment, and adaptable to different environments. However, several challenges remain in practical applications. First, multimodal sensor data exhibit clear differences in signal forms and feature distributions, which makes effective multimodal fusion difficult. Second, differences in motion habits and body characteristics across subjects often lead to performance degradation in cross-subject settings. In addition, most existing IMU-based pose reconstruction methods focus on estimating the current pose and lack the ability to model future motion changes.

To address these issues, this thesis studies two tasks: human activity recognition and 2D human pose reconstruction and prediction. For multimodal human activity recognition, a multimodal fusion method named WIHAR is proposed to address the difficulties in heterogeneous feature alignment between IMU and WiFi data as well as the limited generalization ability in cross-subject scenarios. Specifically, cross-modal contrastive learning is used to map IMU kinematic features and WiFi radio-frequency sensing features into a shared semantic space, and a cross-attention mechanism is introduced to model feature interaction between the two modalities. On this basis, a prototype-based few-shot adaptation strategy is further adopted to alleviate cross-subject distribution shifts by aligning target-user features with class prototypes learned from the source domain. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset show that WIHAR achieves an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06\% in the multimodal activity recognition task. In cross-subject experiments, the initial recognition accuracy is 72.57\%, which can be improved to 90.99\% after fine-tuning with a small amount of target-user data.

For 2D human pose reconstruction and prediction, an autoregressive method named AIPose is proposed to address the lack of temporal prediction ability in existing approaches. The method uses only IMU signals as input and builds a closed-loop architecture consisting of an IMU feature extraction module, a pose reconstruction module, and an IMU prediction module. Historical IMU sequences are used to predict future IMU signals, which are then fed back to estimate future poses, so that current pose reconstruction and future pose prediction can be performed within a unified framework. Experimental results show that, in the 2D keypoint coordinate space, AIPose achieves a mean per-joint position error of 50.56 for current pose reconstruction and 56.29 for future pose prediction on the XRFV2 dataset.
