    With the proliferation of smart wearable devices and wireless sensing technologies, the demand for daily health monitoring is growing. As key sensing components, Inertial Measurement Units (IMU) and WiFi have demonstrated great potential in the field of human sensing due to their low hardware cost, easy accessibility, and privacy-friendliness. However, in practical applications, single sensing modalities face significant limitations: IMUs lack spatial awareness of the environment, while WiFi signals are susceptible to environmental noise interference and struggle to capture subtle limb movements. Furthermore, significant differences in physical characteristics and data distribution between the two modalities pose challenges for deep multimodal fusion. Additionally, existing human pose reconstruction methods are mostly limited to the static restoration of the current pose, lacking the capability to predict future poses. Therefore, targeting the two key problems of the difficulty in deep fusion of heterogeneous multimodal data and the lack of predictability in human pose reconstruction, this thesis conducts research on a multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition method and an autoregressive-based human pose reconstruction and prediction method.

    For the human activity recognition task, addressing the issues of large differences in physical attributes, difficulty in alignment, and deep fusion of IMU and WiFi data, this thesis proposes a multimodal fusion-based human activity recognition method (WICTIVITY). The method adopts Mamba as the feature extractor, utilizing its linear computational complexity to efficiently capture temporal features in long-sequence sensor data. Simultaneously, to overcome the semantic gap between heterogeneous data, a cross-modal contrastive learning strategy is designed to explicitly align the dynamic features of the IMU and the spatial field features of WiFi in a latent semantic space, combined with a cross-attention mechanism to achieve deep feature complementarity. On this basis, a multi-task classification network is constructed to achieve collaborative sensing of human activity, user identity, and scene within a unified framework. Experimental results on the XRFV2 dataset demonstrate that WICTIVITY effectively mines complementary multimodal information, achieving an activity recognition accuracy of 96.06\%, and identity and scene recognition accuracies of 99.93\% and 99.99\% respectively.

    For the human pose reconstruction task, addressing the lack of dynamic prediction capability in existing methods, this thesis proposes an autoregressive mechanism-based human pose reconstruction and prediction method (AIPose), aiming to extend human pose reconstruction from a static mapping to a dynamic temporal generation problem. This method constructs a closed-loop generative architecture comprising IMU feature extraction, pose reconstruction, and IMU prediction. By feeding the predicted future IMU data back into the IMU feature extractor, the model achieves continuous reconstruction and anticipatory prediction of human poses. Experimental results indicate that AIPose achieves centimeter-level pose reconstruction on the XRFV2 dataset and possesses stable long-term pose prediction capabilities. The mean position error for the reconstructed pose at the current moment is 49.35 mm, and the prediction error for future poses is 55.19 mm.
